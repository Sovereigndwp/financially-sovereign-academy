# Trump Accounts: A Family Decision Workbook

**Financially Sovereign Academy · A Sovereign Academy property · Workbook v1.0 · 24 August 2026**


**What this is.** Eight worksheets. No secret information — everything the worksheets rest on is published free at `financiallysovereign.academy/kits/trump-account-family-decision-guide`, with every source linked. What you are holding is the structure that turns that information into a decision you actually made, in writing, with your reasoning next to it, so that in two years you can look back and know what you were thinking.

**What this is not.** Not financial, investment, legal, or tax advice, and not a recommendation to open, fund, or skip anything. Not affiliated with the U.S. government, the Treasury, or the IRS. No one here can see your situation. Where a worksheet tells you to go ask a professional, that is the worksheet working correctly.

**How to use it.** Print it. Sit down with the other adult who has a say, if there is one. Work through Worksheet 1 first — it is the only one that decides anything. Worksheets 2 through 8 support that decision or come after it. Expect forty-five minutes for the first pass. Use pencil.

**A note on what is settled and what is not.** As of this printing, some rules are law, some are proposed, and some are simply unanswered. Every worksheet marks which is which. Anything marked **PROPOSED** can still change. Worksheet 8 lists the source behind every factual claim in this workbook so you can check any of it yourself, and re-check it later.

# Worksheet 1 — Is this right for us?

**Decide this one before anything else. The $1,000 is not what you are deciding about.**

Claiming the $1,000 pilot contribution and *adding your own money* are two different decisions. This worksheet is about the second one. Most families should treat the first as straightforward and the second as genuinely open.

## Step 1. Separate the two decisions

- Have we claimed, or will we claim, the $1,000 pilot contribution? **Yes / No / Not eligible**
- Today's question is only this: **do we put our own money in?** (circle) **Yes · No · Not yet**

## Step 2. The lock-up, in our own words

The money cannot come out before **1 January of the year the child turns 18**, with narrow exceptions. Write what that means for your family specifically.

- The child is ____ years old now. That is roughly **______ years** of no access.
- If our household hit a serious emergency in that window, would we wish this money back? **Yes / No / Unsure**
- Money we would be putting in that we might otherwise need: $ __________

## Step 3. The tax timing, in our own words

Growth is **tax-deferred, not tax-free.** Under traditional IRA treatment, withdrawals are generally taxed as ordinary income when they come out, and a 10% early-distribution charge can apply until the usual retirement-age conditions are met.

# Worksheet 3 — The $5,000 sheet

**One page, one calendar year. Everything that counts toward the limit, in one place, so nobody finds out in December.**

The regular contribution limit is **$5,000 per calendar year**, indexed for inflation after 2027. The mistake families make is not knowing what counts.

## What counts, and what does not

**Counts toward the $5,000:**

- What you contribute
- What relatives and friends contribute
- **What an employer contributes — up to $2,500 per employee per year, and it sits *inside* the $5,000, not on top of it**

**Does not count toward the $5,000:**

- The $1,000 government pilot contribution
- Other government contributions
- Qualified charitable contributions
- Rollovers into the account

## Two things nobody announced

Both of these are unresolved as of this printing, and both affect this page.

- **Proration for 2026.** The program began 4 July 2026. No proration of the annual limit has been announced. Note what you assumed: \hrulefill
- **Carryforward.** The law provides no carryforward of unused limit. Unused room does not roll to next year.

## When a relative wants to contribute

**Gift tax, usually not an issue.** Under a June 2026 IRS revenue procedure, cash contributions to a Trump Account for a beneficiary under 18 count as completed gifts of present interests eligible for the annual gift tax exclusion — which removes a Form 709 filing requirement the account's own restrictions would otherwise have raised. The relief is conditional. It applies where the donor's only taxable gifts are cash contributions to Trump Accounts, the contribution is made before the beneficiary turns 18, total gifts to that beneficiary stay within the annual exclusion ($19,000 for 2026), no gift or GST tax is owed, and the donor is not otherwise required to file a gift tax return that year. Outside those limits, ordinary gift tax rules apply.

- Any single donor giving this child more than the annual exclusion this year? **Yes / No** — if yes, that donor should talk to a tax professional before giving.

**If the relative lives outside the United States, stop and get advice first.** The safe harbor above is written for donors who are natural persons; for donors who are not U.S. persons, the gift tax treatment of cash transfers is not fully settled and is highly fact-specific. This is not a question to settle with a rule of thumb.

- Is any prospective contributor a non-U.S. person or living abroad? **Yes / No**
- If yes, who we will ask for cross-border advice before they contribute: \hrulefill

# Worksheet 5 — Talking to your children

**Three short conversations, ordered by the child's age. None of them is a lecture, and none of them should last more than a few minutes.**

The account is in the child's name. At some point it becomes the child's money and the child's decision. What you say now shapes whether that goes well.

## If the child is under 8 — the "it exists" conversation

Goal: they know it exists and that it is not for now. Nothing else.

- Say: "There is an account with your name on it. It is for when you are grown up. We are not going to use it before then."
- **Do not** say a dollar amount to a young child. It becomes a number they repeat and compare.
- One question to ask them: "What do you think grown-ups use money for?" Then listen without correcting.

Date we had this: __________ · How it went: \hrulefill

## If the child is 8 to 13 — the "waiting is the point" conversation

Goal: they understand that the money is locked, and why locked is not the same as punished.

- Say: "It has to sit there until you are eighteen. That is the rule of the account, not our rule."
- Ask: "What is something you waited a long time for and were glad you waited?" Connect it to that.
- **Do not** attach it to behavior. It is not a reward and it should never be usable as a threat.
- Fair to share: that it grows, and that growing takes years, not weeks.

Date we had this: __________ · What they asked: \hrulefill

## If the child is 14 to 17 — the "this becomes yours" conversation

Goal: they can explain the account back to you, including the tax part. This is the one that matters.

- Ask them to explain it to you. If they say "I get the money at 18," correct it: the money becomes *theirs* under retirement-account rules, taxes apply when it comes out, and there can be an extra charge for taking it early.
- Ask: "What would you want to do with it?" Do not judge the answer. Write it down.
  Their answer: \hrulefill
- Say the true thing: "This will be your decision, not ours. We would rather you know how it works before then than find out after."
- Tell them where the records are. Worksheet 7 is that record.

Date we had this: __________ · Can they explain the tax treatment back? **Yes / Not yet**

## What not to do, at any age

- Do not present it as "free money from the government." It sets up a bad reaction later when they learn about the taxes.
- Do not promise what it will be worth. Nobody knows.
- Do not use it as leverage in an argument. Once you have done that once, it is a bargaining chip forever.

Write the sentence you would want your child to remember about this account. Put it on Worksheet 7 too.

# Worksheet 6 — When something goes wrong

**Delays and rejections are widespread and usually fixable. Work down this list before concluding you do not qualify.**

## First: is it actually a problem, or is it just slow?

Delays were widely reported through 2026 — families told about ten days, then up to roughly four weeks. A wait is not evidence that anything is wrong with your claim.

- Date we submitted Form 4547: __________
- Weeks elapsed: ______ · Is that beyond four weeks? **Yes / No**

## The checklist

Work top to bottom. Tick what you have confirmed.

- [ ] **The child's Social Security number is correct on the form.** Digit by digit, against the card.
- [ ] **The child is a U.S. citizen with a valid SSN, born 2025–2028.** These are the requirements that attach to the child.
- [ ] **The form was not attached to an amended return.** Form 4547 cannot be attached to a Form 1040-X. If you already filed, it goes in separately.
- [ ] **Identity verification is complete.** There is an ID.me verification step. An incomplete verification looks exactly like a delay.
- [ ] **Names match the SSA record exactly** — including hyphens, two last names, and suffixes.
- [ ] **We used only official addresses**, typed by hand: `irs.gov/trumpaccounts` and `trumpaccounts.gov`.

## If you do not file a tax return

Not filing does not exclude you. Form 4547 can be submitted on its own, with the identity-verification step. Do not let a preparer tell you otherwise without showing you where it says so.

## If you file with an ITIN, or you are a mixed-status family

The requirements that are actually published attach to the **child**: U.S. citizen, valid SSN, born 2025–2028. Official application guidance contemplates a parent filing with an ITIN. In the published guidance reviewed for this workbook, there is no requirement about a parent's immigration status. That is stated deliberately and narrowly, rather than repeating the stronger version circulating in some coverage, because the stronger version is not an official IRS position, and regulations are still arriving.

If your situation is complicated, this is a good moment to speak with a qualified tax professional or an accredited immigration legal service, rather than relying on any website — including ours.

## What is settled, what is proposed, what is unanswered

**Settled as of this printing:** the $1,000 pilot contribution and who it attaches to; the $5,000 annual limit; the $2,500 employer figure and that it sits inside the limit; the lock-up date; tax-deferred rather than tax-free treatment; Form 4547 and the absence of a published deadline; the gift tax safe harbor and its conditions.

**Proposed, not final:** four additions to the investment rules published 21 August 2026, comments open until 20 October 2026 — the ESG-index exclusion, the 90% U.S.-company safe harbor, the Investment Company Act registration requirement, and the definition of leverage. The fee cap, the index requirement and the sector exclusion are statute, not proposal.

**Unanswered:** whether 2026 is prorated; how these accounts are treated for college financial aid; several details that regulations have not yet reached.
