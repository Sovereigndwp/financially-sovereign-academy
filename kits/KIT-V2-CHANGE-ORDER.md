# Trump Accounts Client Communication Kit — freshness audit and change order

**Audited:** 2026-08-24 · **Kit version audited:** "Updated July 26, 2026"
**Files:** `TrumpAccountsClientHandoutEN.pdf`, `CuentasTrumpGuiaClienteES.pdf`, `TrumpAccountsClientFAQ-EN-ES.pdf`
**Verdict:** revise and reissue as v2. No claim in the kit is wrong. Three are now framed

---

## What triggered this

**Guidance on Eligible Investments for Trump Accounts.** IRS and Treasury, proposed rule,
published in the Federal Register **2026-08-21**, RIN 1545-BS14.
https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/21/2026-17123/guidance-on-eligible-investments-for-trump-accounts
**Comments close 2026-10-20.** No public hearing scheduled; requests invited.

What it proposes for the growth period (account establishment through 31 December of the year
the beneficiary turns 17):

- The fund must be a domestic corporation, including a regulated investment company, registered
  as a mutual fund or ETF under the Investment Company Act of 1940.
- It must seek to replicate the performance of an index, and hold investments reasonably expected
  to accomplish that.
- No borrowings, derivatives or other strategies used in a way that materially increases the risk
  of loss.
- **Annual fees and annual expenses may not exceed 0.1 percent of the net value of its assets.**
- The index must comprise equity investments in primarily US companies, with a 90 percent US
  company safe harbor, and may not be industry or sector specific, and may not track ESG criteria.

**The headline finding: you were right before they were.** Your 26 July kit already told families
"diversified U.S. stock index funds with expense ratios of 0.10% or less, no other choices." The
August proposal confirms that. Very little published material got there first, and that is worth
knowing when you talk to a partner.

## Defect 1 — proposed rules presented as settled rules. HIGH PRIORITY

This is a credibility defect rather than a factual one, and it is the one a CPA will catch.

The handout's headline stat block states the investment rules flatly, as though final. As of
21 August they are a **proposed** rule with an open comment period. A tax professional reading
your kit after that date, who has seen the Federal Register notice, will register the mismatch.

**Handout, stat block. Replace:**

> Index funds · INVESTMENTS · Diversified U.S. stock index funds with expense ratios of 0.10% or
> less. No other choices.

**With:**

> Index funds · INVESTMENTS (PROPOSED) · U.S. stock index funds only, with total annual fees and
> expenses of 0.10% or less. Proposed IRS rules, 21 August 2026. Comments close 20 October.

**Spanish, same block. Replace:**

> Fondos indexados · INVERSIONES · Fondos indexados de acciones de EE. UU. con comisiones de
> 0.10% o menos. No hay otras opciones.

**With:**

> Fondos indexados · INVERSIONES (PROPUESTO) · Solo fondos indexados de acciones de EE. UU., con
> comisiones y gastos anuales totales de 0.10% o menos. Reglas propuestas por el IRS el 21 de
> agosto de 2026. El plazo de comentarios cierra el 20 de octubre.

## Defect 2 — "expense ratio" is narrower than what the rule says

The proposed text caps **"annual fees and annual expenses"** at 0.1 percent of net asset value.
"Expense ratio" is the common shorthand but it is not the same thing, and a preparer advising a
client on fund selection could reasonably rely on the narrower reading.

Change every instance of "expense ratios of 0.10% or less" to **"total annual fees and expenses of
0.10% or less"**, and in Spanish "comisiones de 0.10% o menos" to **"comisiones y gastos anuales
totales de 0.10% o menos"**. Appears in the handout stat block, the handout's "honest catch"
section, and the FAQ answer to "Can we choose the investments?".

## Gap — gift tax and Form 709. Add this.

Not an error. The kit does not mention gift tax at all, and there is a settled, favourable rule
that predates your verification date.

**Rev. Proc. 2026-25, issued 29 June 2026.** Cash contributions to a Trump Account for a
beneficiary under 18 are treated as **completed gifts of present interests eligible for the annual
gift tax exclusion**, which removes a Form 709 filing requirement that the account's distribution
restrictions would otherwise have raised. The safe harbor applies where all of the following hold:
the donor's only taxable gifts are cash contributions to Trump Accounts; contributions are made
before the beneficiary turns 18; total gifts to any single beneficiary do not exceed the annual
exclusion, $19,000 for 2026; no gift or GST tax liability arises; and the donor is not otherwise
required to file a gift tax return that year.

Sources: KPMG summary https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/06/rev-proc-2026-25-trump-accounts-safe-harbor.html

**Why this matters commercially:** grandparents and other gifters are the segment your own
expansion plan flags as having higher willingness to pay, and this is the question they ask.

**Proposed FAQ addition, EN:**

> **Q. My parents want to contribute. Does anyone have to file a gift tax return?**
> A. Usually no. Under a June 2026 IRS revenue procedure, cash contributions to a Trump Account
> for a child under 18 count as present-interest gifts eligible for the annual exclusion, which
> removes the filing requirement that would otherwise have applied. The relief is conditional:
> total gifts to that child must stay within the annual exclusion, $19,000 for 2026, the
> contribution must be cash, and the donor must not otherwise need to file. Outside those limits,
> normal gift tax rules apply. Bring the numbers to us before a large gift.

**Proposed FAQ addition, ES:** mirror the above, and keep "declaración de impuestos sobre
donaciones (Formulario 709)" on first use so the term is recognisable.

## The differentiator nobody else is covering

Your Spanish edition already answers "¿Y si los padres no tienen papeles?" better than anything
else published. The adjacent question is unanswered everywhere: **what happens when the
grandparent who wants to contribute lives abroad.**

The safe harbor is written for individual donors who are natural persons. For non-US persons the
gift tax treatment of **cash** transfers is not fully settled and is highly fact specific, and
questions about the situs of the transferred property can arise. The honest answer is that this
needs cross-border advice before funding, not a rule of thumb.

Source: Forbes, Virginia La Torre Jeker, 2026-07-22
https://www.forbes.com/sites/virginialatorrejeker/2026/07/22/irs-clarifies-trump-account-gift-taxes-foreign-donors-must-take-care/

**Proposed FAQ addition, ES first since that is where it belongs:**

> **Q. Un abuelo que vive fuera de Estados Unidos quiere aportar. ¿Se puede?**
> A. Se puede aportar, pero el tratamiento fiscal no está resuelto. El alivio del IRS de junio de
> 2026 está escrito para donantes que son personas naturales, y para personas que no son
> estadounidenses el tratamiento de las transferencias en efectivo no está del todo claro y
> depende mucho de los hechos de cada caso. Antes de que un familiar en el extranjero aporte,
> consúltenos y busque asesoría transfronteriza. No es una pregunta para resolver con una regla
> general.

Mirror in English. **This single answer is content no competitor has**, aimed exactly at the
families your buyer serves, and it is a reason for a bilingual practice to want the kit
specifically rather than any kit.

## Maintenance terms, bounded

Offer these terms rather than an open promise. An unbounded commitment is the wrong thing to put
in front of a partner.

> **What "maintained" means.** Your licence includes material updates for twelve months from
> purchase, at no additional cost.
>
> **What counts as a material change.** A new instrument from the IRS, Treasury or the Department
> of Labor that changes what a client should actually do, or that makes a statement in the client
> material inaccurate. A proposed rule becoming final counts. Commentary, analysis and press
> coverage do not.
>
> **The commitment is a cadence, not a response time.** Sources are reviewed weekly. Where a
> material change is found, a revised kit is issued and licensees are emailed. Where nothing has
> changed, nothing is sent.
>
> **What is not included.** Personalised advice, review of your firm's own client materials, or
> answers about an individual client's situation.

Three sentences of scope, one of cadence, one of exclusion. That is defensible to a firm's
compliance reviewer, and it is a promise that can actually be kept by one person.

# v2.1 CORRECTION — Defect 1 overcorrected. HIGH PRIORITY.

**Raised:** 2026-08-24, during the family-kit audit. **Verified against:** 26 U.S.C. §530A
(uscode.house.gov, prelim edition) and the Federal Register notice of 2026-08-21.

## What went wrong

Defect 1 above told you to relabel the investment rules **(PROPOSED)**. That was right about the
*framing* problem and wrong about the *scope*. Most of the investment regime is not proposed. It
is statute, and has been since §530A was enacted.

A CPA reading v2 now sees a black-letter statutory fee cap described as something that "could
still change." That is the same class of credibility defect as the original, pointed the other
way, and it is the kind a tax professional catches immediately.

## The statute, quoted

26 U.S.C. §530A, on eligible investments:

- **"annual fees and expenses of more than 0.1 percent of the balance of the investment"** — the
  cap is statutory. Note the base: *the balance of the investment*, not net asset value.
- **"tracks the returns of a qualified index"**, and must not use leverage.
- **"comprised of equity investments in primarily United States companies"**.
- **"Such term shall not include any industry or sector-specific index, but may include an index
  based on market capitalization"**.
- The restriction runs **"before the first day of the calendar year in which the account
  beneficiary attains age 18"** — which is the same date the kit already states from the other
  side, as 31 December of the year the beneficiary turns 17.

**ESG is not mentioned anywhere in the statute.**

## What was actually new on 21 August 2026

Only these four, from the proposed rule (RIN 1545-BS14, comments close 2026-10-20):

1. The **ESG exclusion** — the statute never mentions ESG.
2. The **90 percent U.S.-company safe harbor** — the statute says only "primarily."
3. The **Investment Company Act of 1940 registration / domestic corporation** requirement.
4. The **gloss on leverage** — borrowings, derivatives or strategies used in a way that materially
   increases the risk of loss.

## Corrected copy

**Handout stat block, EN. Replace the v2 text with:**

> Index funds · INVESTMENTS · U.S. stock index funds only, with annual fees and expenses no
> greater than 0.10% of the balance, and no industry or sector-specific index. This is in the law
> (26 U.S.C. §530A), not pending. Proposed IRS rules of 21 August 2026 would add an ESG exclusion
> and a 90% U.S.-company safe harbor; comments close 20 October.

**Spanish, same block:**

> Fondos indexados · INVERSIONES · Solo fondos indexados de acciones de EE. UU., con comisiones y
> gastos anuales no mayores al 0.10% del saldo, y sin índices sectoriales. Esto está en la ley
> (26 U.S.C. §530A), no está pendiente. Las reglas propuestas por el IRS el 21 de agosto de 2026
> agregarían una exclusión de índices ESG y un margen de seguridad del 90% de empresas
> estadounidenses; el plazo de comentarios cierra el 20 de octubre.

**Defect 2 stands, with one refinement.** "Total annual fees and expenses" remains the correct
widening of "expense ratio." But the statutory base is **the balance of the investment**; the
proposed rule speaks of net asset value. Where the kit states a base, use the statute's.
