Opportunity Cost Is Everywhere

Editorial brief

Introduces opportunity cost as the foundational economist's tool: the real cost of any choice is the best alternative you set aside, whether the currency is money, time, or attention. Kept concrete and non-technical, with an exercise learners can run on a decision they already made today.

Core question
What are you really giving up when you say yes to something?
Mental model
The true cost of a choice is the best thing you didn’t choose.
Learner outcome
The learner can name the next-best alternative they gave up in a decision, including with time.
Supporting concepts
alternatives, time, decision-making
  1. Opening tensionOpen with a recognizable situation, contradiction, or question. Not a definition.
  2. The common beliefState the misconception this article examines: “The cost of something is the money you hand over for it.”

  4. A concrete scenarioA realistic, numerically simple example from ordinary life.

  6. A boundary caseShow where the idea changes with circumstances. Avoid one universal rule.
  7. Why this mattersConnect the concept to real financial decisions.
  8. Look for this todayOne observation exercise the reader can do before the day ends.
  9. Try it yourselfOne short reflection, comparison, or calculation.
  10. One question to carry with you“What are you really giving up when you say yes to something?”
