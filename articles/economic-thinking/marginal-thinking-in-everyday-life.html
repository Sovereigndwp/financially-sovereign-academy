Marginal Thinking in Everyday Life

Editorial brief

Introduces thinking at the margin, asking about the next hour, the next dollar, the next unit rather than the whole choice at once. Shows how this dissolves false all-or-nothing framing in decisions about work, spending, and saving, with everyday, low-stakes examples.

Core question
Is the real question whether to do something, or how much of it?
Mental model
Most good decisions are about the next unit, not the whole thing.
Learner outcome
The learner can turn an all-or-nothing question into a 'how much more or less' question.
Supporting concepts
one more unit, diminishing returns, decisions
