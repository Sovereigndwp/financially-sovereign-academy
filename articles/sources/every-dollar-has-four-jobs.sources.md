**Note on the boundary-case comparisons.** The article's boundary section says a
high-rate debt is a guaranteed cost, that a match on retirement contributions is an
immediate gain, and that a person with no cushion can end up in new, more expensive debt
after a surprise. These are stated as directional relationships a reader can check against
their own numbers, with no rate, threshold, or statistic quoted. They are arithmetic
relationships, not Tier B claims. If a future revision quotes a specific rate, match
percentage, or survey figure, that figure becomes Tier B and must be dated and sourced
(for example to the Federal Reserve's Survey of Household Economics and Decisionmaking).
