Why We Buy Things We Don’t Need

Editorial brief

Looks at the everyday machinery, one-click checkout, targeted ads, social comparison, stress, that turns wanting into buying. Frames overspending as a predictable response to a designed environment, not a character flaw, and gives learners friction they can add back on their own side.

Core question
Why do smart, careful people still buy things they later regret?
Mental model
Spending is designed to be easy; wanting is manufactured on purpose.
Learner outcome
The learner can spot the moment a purchase is being engineered rather than freely chosen.
Supporting concepts
marketing, identity, friction
