Why Prices Matter More Than Cost

Editorial brief

Draws the line between price (what you pay at the register) and cost (what the choice takes from you over its whole life). Uses ordinary examples, cheap shoes, a low monthly payment, the nearest store, to show why the lowest price sometimes carries the highest cost, without pretending the expensive option is always right.

Core question
Why can the lower price sometimes be the more expensive choice?
Mental model
A low price and a low cost are not always the same.
Learner outcome
The learner can separate the sticker price of a choice from what it costs over time.
Supporting concepts
total cost of ownership, tradeoffs, value
  1. Opening tensionOpen with a recognizable situation, contradiction, or question. Not a definition.

  7. Why this mattersConnect the concept to real financial decisions.
  8. Look for this todayOne observation exercise the reader can do before the day ends.
  9. Try it yourselfOne short reflection, comparison, or calculation.
  10. One question to carry with you“Why can the lower price sometimes be the more expensive choice?”
