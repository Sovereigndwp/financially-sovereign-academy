Why Small Decisions Compound

Editorial brief

Explains compounding as a shape, not a formula: repeated small actions grow slowly, then steeply, which is why they feel useless early and decisive late. Applies it to both savings and debt so learners see the same curve working for and against them, using simple, verifiable math.

Core question
Why does the same small habit feel pointless at first and powerful later?
Mental model
Small, repeated choices don’t add up, they multiply.
Learner outcome
The learner can recognize where compounding is working for or against them, in money and in habits.

habits, time, feedback loops
