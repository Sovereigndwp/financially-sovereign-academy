Budgeting & Emergency Savings for Reentry

A paper-first financial decision-making module for correctional and reentry settings. When money is short, the skill is slowing the damage, protecting the next step, asking early, and keeping one option open.

Paper-first Facility-ready Hybrid-supported Pilot-ready, not proven
Provisional pilot-ready draft. External counsel review pending. Not for final facility distribution until legal/privacy feedback is reconciled.

Who it is for

Correctional facilities

Jails and programs that run reentry education on paper, with no internet, phones, or calculators.

Reentry organizations

Nonprofits supporting people in the first months after release.

Workforce & reentry partners

Workforce boards and partners framing financial stabilization for employment readiness.

Funders & institutional reviewers

Foundations, cities, and CDFIs evaluating a pilot.

What learners practice

  • A first-week money map: irregular money in, stacked money out, on one sheet.
  • Bill and card prioritization: sort real pressures into pay now / negotiate / later, and defend the order.
  • The conversations before damage grows: the early ask, in writing, and answering a family money ask without burning the relationship.
  • Emergency and shock planning: a personal definition of an emergency and a realistic first cushion rung.
  • Traps, fees, and fast-money pressure: picking the least expensive option from realistic fast-money offers and saying why.

Four-session structure

About 90 minutes each, standalone, plus an optional capstone.

Session 1
Where the Money Goes. See irregular money honestly on a one-week map.
Session 2
Plan to the Floor. A plan that survives a good week and a bad week.
Session 3
The Conversations. The early ask, in writing; answering a family money ask.
Session 4
Shocks, Traps, and the Plan. Emergencies, fast-money traps, true cost, a plan that holds.
Optional
Capstone: The First 30 Days. Consolidation, usable as a standalone review.
