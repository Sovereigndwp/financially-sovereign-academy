Banking & Credit Rebuilding

A 6-week reentry financial education program focused on safe banking access, credit reports, debt decisions, and avoiding predatory financial products.

For facilitators For learners and participants

Who this is for

This program is for the people and organizations working through the first money barriers after incarceration.

  • Returning citizens preparing for release or recently released.
  • People on probation or parole who want practical money skills.
  • Correctional facilities, jails, and reentry nonprofits running live classes.
  • Workforce programs and probation or parole partners.
  • Funders supporting reentry and financial stability.

It works even if a participant does not yet have an ID, a phone, housing, a job, or a bank account. The class meets people where they are.

What participants learn

  • Why a bank account is usually safer and cheaper than cash and check cashers.
  • How to open an account, including with limited ID or a flagged history, using second-chance and Bank On certified accounts and credit unions.
  • How to read a credit report and understand what a credit score is and is not.
  • Safe ways to start rebuilding credit, like secured cards used and paid on time.
  • How to spot and avoid payday lenders, check cashers, and rent-to-own.
  • First steps for old debt, collections, and court-ordered payments, with referral to free legal help.
  • A simple 30-day money setup plan they can actually follow.

Two ways to use the program

The same six-week curriculum comes in two forms. They work together. The facility version teaches and plans. The learner version helps people take real steps after release.

🏛️ Facility-led version

For live, facilitator-led classes inside jails, prisons, probation or parole programs, and reentry organizations.

  • Paper-first. No internet or phones required.
  • Printable workbook and assessments.
  • Safe for facility security review.
  • No live bank applications and no sharing of private financial details in class.

📱 Learner workbook version

For participants to work through with a facilitator, or on their own after release when they may have a phone.

  • Plain-language workbook that can be printed.
  • Step-by-step worksheets and a 30-day plan.
  • No internet needed to use the printed workbook.
  • Can be led by a facilitator or used independently.

A simple look at the six lessons

  • Week 1: Why a bank account beats cash.
  • Week 2: Opening an account, even with little ID or a record.
  • Week 3: How credit reports and scores really work.
  • Week 4: Safe ways to start building credit.
  • Week 5: Old debt, collections, and court debt.
  • Week 6: Your 30-day money setup plan.
This workbook can be printed, or a facilitator can use it with a group. Nothing here asks you to go online or share private financial details.

Six-week outline

About 90 minutes per session. The schedule can be compressed into fewer longer sessions or stretched into more short ones to fit a facility.

Week 1. Why banking beats cash. The real cost of check cashers. Banks and credit unions. What FDIC and NCUA insurance mean.
Week 2. Opening an account with a record or little ID. Documents you need and how to get them. What ChexSystems is. Second-chance and Bank On certified accounts.
Week 3. How credit really works. Credit report versus credit score. What moves a score. How to get a free report.
Week 4. Rebuilding credit step by step. Secured cards and credit-builder tools. On-time payments. Avoiding credit repair scams.
Week 5. Old debt, collections, and court debt. The three kinds of debt. First steps for each. Free legal help for court fines, fees, restitution, and child support.
Week 6. Your 30-day money setup plan. Put it together: ID, account, one credit step. Avoid the common traps. Know who to call.

Facilitator notes

A few things that make this program work in a reentry setting.

  • You do not need to be a finance expert. You need to be prepared, honest, and respectful.
  • Co-facilitation with someone who has lived reentry experience builds trust. This is our design choice, supported in general reentry practice, and not a measured outcome.
  • In class, keep things general. People should never write or say account numbers, Social Security numbers, PINs, balances, or case numbers.
  • When a question becomes personal, point to a vetted local partner. The class is education, not personal advice.
  • The full scripts, timings, and activities are in the facilitator guide above.

Important boundaries

This program is education and preparation. It is not advice.

  • Educational content only.
  • Not financial advice.
  • Not legal advice.
  • Not tax advice.
  • No private financial information should be shared in class.
  • We do not promise account approval, a credit score, or a specific drop in recidivism.
  • For personal needs, we connect people to qualified local partners.
