# Spanish Adaptation Brief: {{ARTICLE TITLE}}

Localization happens **after** the English article is approved, never before. This brief
prepares the Spanish version; it does not itself translate line by line. The goal is a
version that reads as if it were written in Spanish for its readers, not a word-for-word
rendering.

- **Source article ID:** FSA-ARTICLE-NNN (English, approved)
- **Source slug:** {{english-slug}}
- **Target path:** /es/articulos/{{slug-or-localized-slug}}.html
- **Metadata:** set `translationOf: FSA-ARTICLE-NNN`, `language: es`, and add `es` to
  the source article's `availableLanguages`. The Spanish record is its own entry that
  points back to the English source.

## What must be preserved
- **Concept and misconception.** The idea and the belief examined stay identical.
- **Sequence.** The 13-part order is unchanged.
- **Mental model.** Re-express it in natural Spanish; it must mean the same thing and
  stay one memorable line.
- **Dignity.** The respectful, non-shaming stance carries over exactly.
- **Factual meaning.** Every Tier B claim keeps its meaning and its source. Do not soften
  or sharpen a number in translation.
- **Formality (register).** Choose and hold a consistent register (usted vs tú) suited to
  the audience, and keep it throughout.

## What needs localization review
Flag each of these for the translator and a local reviewer:
- **Examples and scenarios.** Currency, everyday costs, and settings that assume U.S.
  life may need local equivalents. Note whether the reader is Spanish-speaking US (U.S.
  institutions usually stay) or Colombia/LATAM (local institutions and currency).
- **Institutions and agencies.** U.S. agencies (CFPB, FDIC, IRS) may need a local
  counterpart, or a note that the rule is U.S.-specific. Do not silently swap a U.S. law
  for a foreign one; re-source it.
- **Idioms and phrasing.** Any English idiom that will not translate cleanly.
- **Legal or program specifics.** Anything that differs by country and must be
  re-checked against local authorities.

## Deliverable
A localization plan listing, part by part, what carries over unchanged and what needs a
local example, a re-sourced fact, or a translator's judgment. The Spanish version is a
new draft: it enters at `drafted` / `human-review-required` and goes through review in
Spanish. The skill does not approve or publish it.
