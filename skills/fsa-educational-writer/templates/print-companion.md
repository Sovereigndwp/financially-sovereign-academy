# Print Companion: {{ARTICLE TITLE}}

A print companion is the article made usable with a pencil and no internet. It must
read fully offline, work in black and white, and require no account, app, login, or
private financial data. It suits facilitators, correctional and reentry settings, and
anyone without reliable connectivity. The base article page already prints via the
shared print stylesheet, so the record's `formats` already includes `print`; this
companion is an additional facilitator artifact and does not change the `formats` field.

- **Article ID:** FSA-ARTICLE-NNN
- **Slug:** {{slug}}
- **Format:** print (black and white, offline, pencil-and-paper)

## 1. Print-friendly article
The full article text, laid out for paper. Keep the 13 parts intact and readable in a
single ink color. Remove interactive prompts to "click" or "go online." Keep the
education-only footer: "Created by Dalia. financiallysovereign.academy. Educational
content only. Not financial, legal, tax, or investment advice." Include the article ID
and a revision date in the print footer.

## 2. Learner reflection
Two or three open prompts drawn from the article's mental model and its
carry-with-you question. Blank lines for handwritten answers. No prompt asks for real
balances, debts, criminal history, or personal financial data.

## 3. Short exercise
One pencil-and-paper task built from the article's "Try it yourself," restated so it
needs nothing but the page. If it involves arithmetic, use round numbers and show one
worked example so a learner can follow the method. Provide the blank space to work in.

## 4. Facilitator note
A short note for whoever hands out the page: the one idea to land, the misconception to
watch for, one or two questions to open a discussion, and a reminder to keep the tone
free of shame and to name structural constraints without removing agency. Note the
audience adaptations that apply (for example reentry barriers, or youth legal
differences).

## 5. Answer guidance
Not an answer key that implies one right answer, but guidance on what a good reflection
looks like, the range of reasonable responses, and the reasoning to steer toward. For
any calculation, show the worked steps and the result. Make clear that the scenario is
an FSA illustration, not a data point, and that any general rule should be checked
against the learner's own numbers.

---
**Reminder:** no em dashes, no decorative emoji, and nothing that assumes a device or a
connection. The print companion is a draft like any other output; the skill does not
approve or publish it.
