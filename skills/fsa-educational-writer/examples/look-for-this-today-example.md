# Example: a good "Look for this today" observation exercise

An illustration of the observation-exercise technique, not reusable text. Concept:
incentives (follow the incentive before you judge the person). Write a fresh one for each
article.

## The exercise
> Sometime today, notice one moment where someone is trying to help you decide something
> about money: a cashier offering the store card at checkout, an app suggesting an upgrade,
> a sign promising a discount if you sign up. Before you decide anything, ask one quiet
> question: what does this person or company get if I say yes? You do not have to act on
> the answer or turn anything down. Just watch how the offer looks a little different once
> you can see the incentive behind it.

## Why it works
- **Observation, not action.** It asks the learner to notice, not to change behavior or
  buy or refuse anything.
- **Offline and account-free.** It needs no app, no bank account, no internet, and no
  private data. A person can do it just by paying attention during an ordinary day.
- **It rehearses the article's mental model** in the wild, so the idea gets tested in the
  learner's own life rather than accepted on the article's say-so.
- **Low stakes and non-judgmental.** Noticing an incentive is not the same as accusing
  anyone of bad intent, and the exercise is careful to say so.

## What to copy from this (the technique, not the words)
- Frame it as "notice," not "do."
- Keep it doable before bedtime with nothing but attention.
- Tie it directly to the article's one idea.
- Require no device, account, or disclosure.
- No em dashes, no emoji.
