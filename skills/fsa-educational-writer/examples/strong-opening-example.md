# Example: a strong opening tension vs a weak definitional opening

This is an illustration of technique, not text to paste into an article. It uses a
different concept (opportunity cost) than the prototype so no one mistakes it for
reusable copy. Write your own opening for each article.

## Weak: opening with a definition
> Opportunity cost is the value of the next-best alternative that a person gives up
> when making a choice. Economists consider it a foundational concept in decision-making.
> In this article we will explore opportunity cost and how it applies to everyday life.

Why it is weak: it leads with the term, tells the reader what to think before they care,
and reads like a textbook. There is no tension and nothing at stake. The reader has no
reason to keep going, and the word "explore" plus the phrase "in this article we will"
is filler.

## Strong: opening with a tension from ordinary life
> You had a free Saturday and two ways to spend it. You could pick up an extra shift for
> a hundred dollars, or you could help a friend move and get nothing but lunch. You took
> the shift. The hundred dollars was real, and it felt like the obvious call.
>
> A week later the friend didn't ask you to their housewarming, and it stung more than
> you expected. That is when a quiet question showed up: the shift paid a hundred dollars,
> but what did it actually cost?

Why it works: it drops the reader into a concrete moment with a real choice and a small
ache. The idea (that the true cost of a yes is the best thing you gave up) is about to
land on its own. The term "opportunity cost" is not named yet. It will be named later,
after the reader has felt it.

## What to copy from this (the technique, not the words)
- Start in a specific moment, not a category.
- Give the reader a choice they recognize.
- End the opening on a fairer question than the one people usually ask themselves.
- Save the technical term for after the feeling.
- No em dashes, no emoji, plain words.
