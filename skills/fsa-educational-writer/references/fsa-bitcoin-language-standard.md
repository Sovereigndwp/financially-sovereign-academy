# FSA Bitcoin Language Standard

FSA is a financial-education library, not a Bitcoin promotion. Most articles will not
mention Bitcoin at all. When Bitcoin does belong in an article, the language must be
precise, and it must never drift into promotion, prediction, or blurring Bitcoin with
other assets.

## Capitalization
- **Bitcoin** (capital B) refers to the network, the protocol, and the asset as a
  whole. "Bitcoin is a payment network."
- **bitcoin** (lowercase b) refers to units of the currency. "She saved a small amount
  of bitcoin." "The fee was a fraction of a bitcoin."

## Distinctions that must stay sharp
- **Bitcoin is not a synonym for "crypto."** Do not use "crypto" as a stand-in for
  Bitcoin, and do not lump Bitcoin together with other cryptocurrencies as if they were
  one category. If an article needs to discuss other cryptocurrencies, name them as a
  separate thing.
- **Self-custody vs custodial.** Holding your own keys is different from holding an IOU
  at an exchange. If the distinction is relevant, state it plainly.
- **Price volatility vs protocol risk.** The price moving is a market fact; the protocol
  failing is a different (and much rarer) kind of risk. Do not conflate them.
- **Saving vs trading.** Buying and holding over a long horizon is a different activity
  from short-term trading on price moves. Keep them separate.
- **Bitcoin vs a guaranteed investment.** Bitcoin is not a savings account and not a
  guaranteed return. Never imply it is.

## Honesty rules
- Acknowledge risk plainly. Bitcoin can lose value, and it can lose a lot of value.
- **No price predictions.** Never forecast a future price or direction.
- **No return promises.** Never suggest a guaranteed or expected gain.
- Stay inside FSA's educational-neutrality boundary: explain what Bitcoin is and the
  tradeoffs involved, do not tell a specific reader to buy, sell, hold, or custody it a
  particular way. FSA is mission-driven for the underbanked and is explicitly not a
  Bitcoin custody or intake funnel; routing to advice or custody products belongs to
  BSA or a qualified adviser, not here.

## When Bitcoin belongs, and when it does not
Include Bitcoin only where it genuinely improves the lesson. For example, an article on
inflation and purchasing power may note, briefly and neutrally, that some people hold
Bitcoin as one response to currency debasement, presented as one option among several,
with its risks stated and no endorsement. Do not bolt Bitcoin onto an article where it
does not serve the concept, and do not turn a general money lesson into a Bitcoin essay.
If Bitcoin is not making the lesson clearer, leave it out.
