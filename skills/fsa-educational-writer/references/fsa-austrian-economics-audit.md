# FSA Austrian Economics Audit (fourth reviewer, draft review gate)

A concept-integrity review that reads a Money Ideas brief or draft as an economist in the
Austrian tradition would, and recommends improvements to the **teaching of the economics**.

It is a reviewer, not an author, and not a school advocate.

## Why this reviewer exists

The draft review gate in `TSA/standards/content-operating-system.md` runs three independent
reviews: educational-effectiveness, Bitcoin technical audit, and content-and-context logic.
For FSA Money Ideas, the Bitcoin slot is almost always not applicable, so in practice a Money
Ideas article reaches approval with **nobody auditing it as economics**.

That is a real gap, because the library is economics. FSA-ARTICLE-000 is an article about
time preference that never names time preference. FSA-ARTICLE-002 rests entirely on
opportunity cost and marginal choice. The rubric's first dimension is "concept accuracy: a
specialist would not wince," and until now no specialist has read for it.

The Austrian tradition is a good fit for this particular library for a specific reason, not a
partisan one: it is the tradition that works hardest on exactly what FSA teaches. Choice
under scarcity, subjective valuation, opportunity cost as a forgone alternative rather than a
number, time preference, action under genuine uncertainty, and decisions at the margin. These
are the concepts in FSA's own controlled vocabulary.

## Scope

### In scope

The reviewer reads for the correct, precise, and honest teaching of:

| Concept | Core reference | Where it shows up in FSA |
|---|---|---|
| Subjective value: value is a ranking a person makes, not a property of a thing | Menger, *Principles of Economics* (1871) | every article; the foundation of FSA's dignity rule |
| Opportunity cost as the forgone alternative, subjective and forward-looking | Wieser; Buchanan, *Cost and Choice* (1969) | 002, 004, and the whole Economic Thinking series |
| Time preference: present goods are valued over future goods, and the rate differs by person and circumstance | Böhm-Bawerk, *Capital and Interest* | 000, 001, and every saving article |
| Marginal decision-making: choices happen at the margin, over units, not over categories | Menger; Mises, *Human Action* | 002's "next free dollar" |
| Uncertainty distinct from measurable risk | Knight (1921); Mises | emergency funds, insurance, risk articles |
| Prices as carriers of dispersed information | Hayek, "The Use of Knowledge in Society" (1945) | prices, credit, banking articles |
| The time structure of production and capital, including human capital | Böhm-Bawerk | 002's "Grow"; investing articles |
| Entrepreneurial alertness | Kirzner | income and work articles |

For each, the reviewer asks four questions:

1. **Is the concept correct as stated?** Not "is it Austrian," but is it right.
2. **Is the mechanism present, or only the conclusion?** FSA articles frequently assert a
   true thing without the reason underneath it. Supplying the mechanism is this reviewer's
   highest-value contribution.
3. **Is a concept doing load-bearing work while unnamed?** An article that teaches time
   preference without naming it leaves the reader unable to reuse the idea.
4. **Does a precision improvement strengthen the article's own argument?** If yes, recommend
   it. If it only makes the article more technically pleasing, do not.

### Explicitly out of scope

The reviewer **may not** recommend, and the synthesis editor **must** reject:

- **Policy positions of any kind.** Central banking, fractional reserve, monetary regimes,
  regulation, taxation, government spending. FSA teaches households how money decisions work,
  not what monetary system should exist.
- **School-partisan framing.** No "Austrians say," no "mainstream economics is wrong," no
  naming of schools in learner-facing copy at all. The reader learns the mechanism, never the
  faction.
- **Contested theory presented as settled.** Austrian business cycle theory, the calculation
  debate, methodological objections to empirical economics. These are live disputes. Rubric
  dimension 1 requires that a specialist would not wince, and a mainstream specialist would
  wince at these presented as fact.
- **Objections to a source based on who published it.** FSA's source standard prefers
  government and central-bank sources for stable concepts, and FSA-ARTICLE-002 cites Federal
  Reserve Education for opportunity cost. That citation is correct and stays. The reviewer may
  challenge whether a source supports a claim. It may never challenge a source for its
  provenance.
- **Macro framing of a household problem.** A reader deciding whether to cancel a twelve
  dollar subscription is not helped by monetary theory. Scale the answer to the decision.
- **Anything that reduces dignity.** Telling a reader whose constraint is rent that their real
  problem is somewhere upstream is both out of scope and a violation of the voice standard.

### The governing test

> Would this change help a reader make a better decision this month, using only the
> information in front of them?

If yes, recommend it. If it would mainly make the article more theoretically satisfying, or
more aligned with a school, drop it. **Every recommendation must be defensible to a
mainstream economist as simply correct**, not as correct-within-a-tradition. In practice this
is a lower bar than it sounds: subjective and forward-looking opportunity cost, time
preference, marginal analysis and genuine uncertainty are standard economics. The
Austrian-distinctive material is almost entirely in the out-of-scope list, which is the
clearest sign the boundary is drawn in the right place.

## Authority

**Advisory. Findings only.** This reviewer scores nothing, approves nothing, and rewrites
nothing. It feeds the educational review gate, where the synthesis editor reconciles it
against the other reviewers and a human approves. Where it conflicts with the FSA voice
standard, the source standard, the dignity rule, or the 13-part structure, **those win** and
the conflict is recorded rather than resolved by the reviewer.

## When it runs

Twice, and both are cheap:

- **Mode 1, on the editorial brief.** Best value for money. Catching a missing mechanism at
  brief stage costs a sentence; catching it after drafting costs a rewrite.
- **Mode 3, on the finished draft**, as an input to the educational review gate.

Not on published articles unless a claim is challenged.

## Output

```
Austrian Economics Audit
- Concept integrity:   <n> findings (correct / imprecise / missing mechanism / unnamed)
- Strengthens the article's own argument: <n>
- Out of scope, rejected by the reviewer itself: <n>  (list them, so the boundary is visible)
- Conflicts with an FSA standard: <n>  (record, do not resolve)
- Verdict: <sound / precision improvements available / concept error>
```

Every finding carries: the concept, the quoted line, what is missing or imprecise, the
proposed change, and **whether the change makes the article's existing argument stronger or
merely more technical**. Findings of the second kind are recorded and not recommended.

A finding the reviewer rejected as out of scope is still listed. A guardrail nobody can see
working is a guardrail nobody trusts.
