# FSA Educational Review Rubric

Score a draft on each of the ten dimensions from 1 to 5. **Any dimension scoring below 4
requires revision before a human can approve the article.** The skill scores and
recommends; it never approves.

Scale, in general:
- **5** Exemplary. Nothing to fix on this dimension.
- **4** Solid. Minor polish possible; approvable on this dimension.
- **3** Mixed. A real weakness a reader would notice. Revise.
- **2** Weak. The dimension mostly fails. Revise.
- **1** Absent or wrong. Rework from the concept up.

## The ten dimensions

1. **Concept accuracy.** Is the idea correct, and is any technical term used correctly?
   No overclaiming, no subtle errors.
2. **Learner clarity.** Can a grade 7 to 9 reader follow it? Is each idea explained
   before its term is named?
3. **Practical relevance.** Does it connect to real decisions the learner actually
   faces? Would they use it this week?
4. **Mental-model strength.** Is the one-line model reusable, memorable, and true beyond
   the article's own example?
5. **Scenario quality.** Is the scenario ordinary, numerically simple, original, and
   clearly an FSA illustration?
6. **Nuance.** Does the boundary case do real work, including a case where the obvious
   recommendation may not be right? Does it avoid turning the model into a rigid rule?
7. **Evidence quality.** Are Tier B claims sourced and dated, stable concepts summarized
   correctly, and FSA scenarios kept out of the evidence column? Nothing invented.
8. **Tone and dignity.** Does it respect the learner, name structural constraints, keep
   agency, and avoid shame? Voice on target.
9. **Originality.** Framing, scenario, model wording, and exercises are the library's
   own. The originality note holds up.
10. **FSA integration.** Correct series, module names, concepts, audiences, related
    links, education-only footer, and the 13-part structure intact.

## Final writing audit checklist

Run this pass after scoring. Each item is a yes/no check on the draft body. Any "yes"
in the "problem present" sense is a finding to fix.

- Repeated sentence openings (several sentences or paragraphs starting the same way).
- Excessive rhetorical questions (more than one or two in the piece).
- Artificial transitions ("Now let's turn to," "With that in mind").
- Generic conclusions or motivational endings instead of an open carry-with-you
  question.
- Unnecessary headings that chop the flow without adding structure.
- Over-polished symmetry (every paragraph and section the same length and shape).
- Long abstract passages with no concrete anchor nearby.
- Unexplained jargon (a term used before its idea is explained).
- Uniform paragraph length throughout.
- Excessive "you" (direct address that starts to nag).
- Unsupported claims (a Tier B fact with no source, or a stat that appeared from
  nowhere).
- Repeated ideas (the same point made twice in different words).
- Moralizing (telling the reader what a good person would do).
- Hidden assumptions about income or access (assuming a bank account, a smartphone,
  spare cash, or reliable internet).
- **Em dashes** anywhere in the body. There must be none.
- **Decorative emoji** anywhere in the body. There must be none.
- Copied or paraphrased wording from another source.

Record the rubric scores with one-line justifications and the audit results as findings.
If any dimension is below 4 or any audit item is a problem, the recommendation is
revision, and the status stays `drafted` / `human-review-required`.
